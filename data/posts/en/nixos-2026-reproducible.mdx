# "I use NixOS btw." 

It's a meme, but for me, it's a survival strategy. After years of wrestling with "standard" Linux distributions where a system update or a hardware swap could break days of environment tweaking, I decided that my development setup should be as version-controlled as my code.

## The Problem: Accidental State
We've all been there: you spend three days perfecting your Neovim config, installing the right LSP servers, and tweaking your window manager. You feel productive. Then, you switch laptops or a dependency breaks, and you're back at square one. This is "accidental state."

## The Solution: Code as Truth
In my `nixos-config`, my entire infrastructure—from my Steam Deck-like "Cube" to my main production "Server"—is defined in a single Flake.

### 1. Declarative Neovim with NVF
I don't manually manage `.config/nvim`. I use **NVF (Neovim Flake)**. 
- My Rust and Angular environments are imported modules.
- `tabstop`, `shiftwidth`, and even my `tokyonight` theme are declared in Nix.
- **Benefit:** If I move to a new machine, I don't "install" Neovim; I just build my system, and my IDE is *there*, exactly as I left it.

<Alert variant="info">
  **Declarative IDEs:** Using a Flake for Neovim means my editor is a deterministic output of my system configuration. No more `:MasonInstall` or manual plugin management.
</Alert>

### 2. Multi-Host Mastery
My configuration isn't just for one PC. It manages several distinct roles:
- **Server:** Running Traefik, Authelia, and a full media stack via Podman/Quadlet.
- **Clovis & Septimius:** High-performance desktops with shared modules for Rust development.
- **Cube:** A gaming setup using Jovian-NixOS for a SteamOS-like experience on generic hardware.

### 3. Impermanence: The "Clean Slate" Strategy
One of the most powerful patterns I use is the `impermanence` module. My root partition is effectively wiped on every boot. Only what I *explicitly* mark for persistence (`/persist`) survives. 

This forces me to be honest: if I configure something and forget to put it in my Nix code, it disappears on the next reboot. This ensures my config is always 100% complete and documented.

## Why this matters
If you're a developer in 2026, your environment is your craft. Don't let it be a collection of accidents. Make it a declarative, versioned, and reproducible asset.
